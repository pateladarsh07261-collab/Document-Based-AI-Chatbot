Title: Anxiety Awareness and Grounding Strategies
Category: Anxiety Awareness
Source: APA & Mental Health Education Resource

1. What is Everyday Anxiety?
Anxiety is a feeling of worry, nervousness, or unease about uncertain outcomes. Occasional anxiety before exams, interviews, or major life transitions is normal. Persistent anxiety that disrupts daily functioning should be evaluated by a licensed mental health professional.

2. Common Signs of Stress-Related Anxiety
- Cognitive signs: Racing thoughts, overthinking worst-case scenarios, difficulty concentrating.
- Physical signs: Rapid heartbeat, shallow breathing, muscle tightness, restlessness, or stomach discomfort.

3. Immediate Grounding Techniques
- The 5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Method: Identify 5 things you can see, 4 things you can physically touch, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, and 1 thing you can taste. This shifts attention away from anxious thoughts back to the present environment.
- 4-7-8 Calming Breath: Inhale quietly through the nose for 4 seconds, hold the breath for 7 seconds, and exhale completely through the mouth for 8 seconds.
- Cognitive Reframing: Write down the anxious thought, examine objective evidence for and against it, and replace catastrophic predictions with balanced statements.

4. Educational Disclaimer
These techniques support everyday emotional regulation and are educational in nature; they do not replace clinical evaluation or medical diagnosis.
